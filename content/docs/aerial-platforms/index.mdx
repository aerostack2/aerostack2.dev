An aerial platform connects Aerostack2 to one kind of drone or simulator. Everything above it stays
the same, so a mission tested in simulation flies a real drone once you change the platform. How
platforms work, their state machine and their control modes are explained on the
[aerial platform](/docs/concepts/aerial-platform) concept page.

## Available platforms

| Platform | Package | Flies | ROS 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Multirotor simulator](/docs/aerial-platforms/multirotor-simulator) | `as2_platform_multirotor_simulator` | A lightweight simulated multirotor | Humble, Jazzy |
| [Gazebo](/docs/aerial-platforms/gazebo-simulation) | `as2_platform_gazebo` | Simulated drones in 3D worlds with sensors | Humble, Jazzy |
| [Pixhawk](/docs/aerial-platforms/pixhawk) | `as2_platform_pixhawk` | Drones with a PX4 autopilot, over uXRCE-DDS | Humble, Jazzy |
| [MAVLink](/docs/aerial-platforms/mavlink) | `as2_platform_mavlink` | PX4 autopilots over MAVLink, through MAVROS | Humble, Jazzy |
| [Crazyflie](/docs/aerial-platforms/crazyflie) | `as2_platform_crazyflie` | Bitcraze Crazyflie nano quadcopters | Humble |
| [DJI OSDK](/docs/aerial-platforms/dji-osdk) | `as2_platform_dji_osdk` | DJI Matrice 210 V2 and M300 through the Onboard SDK | Humble |
| [DJI PSDK](/docs/aerial-platforms/dji-psdk) | `as2_platform_dji_psdk` | DJI Matrice 300, 350 and M30 through the Payload SDK | Humble |
| [Ryze Tello](/docs/aerial-platforms/ryze-tello) | `as2_platform_tello` | The Ryze Tello over Wi-Fi | Humble, Jazzy |
| [Betaflight](/docs/aerial-platforms/betaflight) | `as2_platform_betaflight` | Flight controllers running Betaflight, over MSP. Deprecated | Humble |

The two simulators ship with Aerostack2. The others live in their own repositories under the
[aerostack2](https://github.com/aerostack2) organization and are built from source in your
workspace, next to Aerostack2.

Crazyflie and both DJI platforms aren't supported on Jazzy yet. The Betaflight platform is
deprecated and no longer maintained.

## Which simulator

Start with the **multirotor simulator**. It needs no 3D engine, starts in a second and runs
anywhere, including macOS. Move to **Gazebo** when a mission needs sensors that see the world, like
cameras and lidars, or a 3D world to fly in.

## Running a platform

Each drone runs its own platform node, named `platform`, in the drone's namespace. Every platform
has a launch file that takes the namespace and its configuration files, for example:

```bash
ros2 launch as2_platform_multirotor_simulator as2_platform_multirotor_simulator.launch.py \
  namespace:=drone0
```

The rest of the stack runs next to it, and two choices depend on the platform:

- The [state estimator](/docs/concepts/state-estimator) plugin. Simulators provide ground truth, while
  real drones usually provide their own odometry.
- The [motion controller](/docs/concepts/motion-controller) plugin, which has to produce a control
  mode the platform accepts.

## Your own drone

If your drone isn't listed, a platform for it is a single node with a handful of methods. See
[writing a platform](/docs/concepts/aerial-platform#writing-a-platform) and the
[platform guide](/docs/develop/aerial-platform).
